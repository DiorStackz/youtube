# VIDEO SCRIPTS

## TEMPLATE STRUCTURE

### HOOK
Direct curiosity.

### SETUP
Explain topic.

### BUILD-UP
Layer facts.

### TWIST
Introduce unknown.

### PAYOFF
Key insight.

### ENDING
Leave question.

## NOTES

- Pattern interrupts every ~20 seconds
- Keep pacing tight

---

# SCRIPT 01 — THE WOW! SIGNAL

## MAIN VERSION (~80 seconds)

> In 1977, a telescope in Ohio picked up a signal from deep space.
>
> It lasted 72 seconds.
>
> Then it vanished.
>
> And almost 50 years later… no one can explain what it was.
>
> The night of August 15th. The Big Ear radio telescope. Scanning automatically. No humans watching.
>
> The next morning, an astronomer named Jerry Ehman opened the printout. Page after page of static. Then… something different.
>
> Six characters. *6EQUJ5.*
>
> A signal so strong, so structured, it broke the chart. Coming from a single point in space — near the constellation Sagittarius.
>
> Ehman grabbed a red pen. Circled it. Wrote one word in the margin.
>
> *Wow.*
>
> That word became the name of the most mysterious signal in the history of astronomy.
>
> A comet? Doesn't fit the data.
> A satellite? No government has ever claimed it.
> Random noise? Too precise. Too clean.
>
> And here's what makes it stranger.
>
> It hit the exact frequency scientists had predicted an alien civilization would use to communicate. The frequency of hydrogen. The universal language of the cosmos.
>
> For 47 years, every telescope on Earth has searched that same patch of sky.
>
> It has never returned.
>
> Whatever sent it… only spoke once.
>
> And no one knows what it said.

## VIRAL SHORT VERSION (~25 seconds)

> In 1977, a telescope picked up a signal from deep space.
>
> It lasted 72 seconds. Then vanished forever.
>
> It hit the exact frequency scientists predicted aliens would use to communicate.
>
> The astronomer who found it grabbed a red pen, circled it, and wrote one word in the margin.
>
> *Wow.*
>
> That signal has never returned. And to this day, no one knows what it was.

## ALTERNATIVE OPENING HOOKS

**Hook 1 — The Discovery Angle:**
> In 1977, a radio telescope heard something it was never supposed to hear. Almost 50 years later — we still don't know what it was.

**Hook 2 — The Tension Angle:**
> There's a single recording, taken in 1977, that scientists have studied for nearly half a century. They still can't explain it. And it has never repeated.

**Hook 3 — The Eerie Angle:**
> A scientist opened a printout one morning, grabbed a red pen, and wrote one word: Wow. That word became the most haunting mystery in modern astronomy.
